### ▫️ A Circuit for In-Context Learning
**-/10** · Andrew Gao、William Gao · arXiv · 2026-11-03 · [原文](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05344v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05344v1)
*入选：能力迁移*

> 📄 **摘要**：In-context learning (ICL) lets a pretrained language model perform a task specified by demonstrations in its prompt, with no parameter updates. With arbitrary labels the model must acquire a mapping it cannot have memorized, so the learning happens within a single forward pass. Prior work found candidate parts but not how they compose. We give the composition for few-shot sentence classification with arbitrary labels in three 3-4B-parameter transformers. The model implements a key-value memory over label-token positions: each label token's residual stream encodes the label's identity, and middle-layer attention heads write into it the class of the preceding demonstration as its key. At the query, upper-layer heads match the query's class against the stored keys and copy out the matching labels. An early-layer signal encoding whether any label has repeated gates this readout; in its absence the readers attend to the start of the prompt and no label is favored. A separate set of heads suppresses the most recently seen label. Each step is established causally by activation patching, head ablations against random-head baselines, and steering with the model's own signals. A softmax model of the readout with two constants per head, fit on one prompt distribution, predicts another. The computation is conserved across the three models, the named heads alone reproduce the behaviour, and a transformer trained from scratch grows the same parts. The circuit transfers to semantic concepts and to labels with meaning, and the same parts, with a causal gate, appear from 0.6B to 8B in one family and survive instruction tuning. ICL in this setting is a gated associative lookup built from a few identifiable component types, traced causally from demonstrations to prediction.

### ▫️ MDSKILL: Learning to Internalize Skill Memory through Multi-View Distillation for Efficient Dynamic Agent Self-Evolution
**-/10** · Dian Jin、Jinluan Yang、Kairong Han、Baohong Li、Zijing Hu、Yi He · arXiv · 2026-11-03 · [原文](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05313v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05313v1)
*入选：RL×LLM方法*

> 📄 **摘要**：Skill memories encode past experiences as textual prompts to improve reinforcement learning (RL) for self-evolving agents. Complementing RL, On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) provides dense, fine-grained supervision to internalize knowledge from skill-guided trajectories and accelerate policy learning. However, self-generated memories often contain noisy supervision and functionally entangled, redundant, or conflicting knowledge, making direct distillation difficult. Under limited training budgets, these issues can hinder knowledge internalization and even degrade performance. To address this problem, we propose MDSKILL, a multi-view distillation approach for skill memory internalization. MDSKILL organizes skill memories into three complementary functional views (state grounding, action planning, and execution control) and selects high-quality skills using dynamic metrics to construct view-specific and integrated reasoning branches. OPSD then transfers and consolidates complementary experiential knowledge across these branches into a shared policy, enabling efficient skill internalization and continuous agent self-evolution. We evaluate MDSKILL on three agent benchmarks against RL, skill-augmented, and self-distillation baselines that do not rely on stronger external teacher models. Without additional computational overhead, MDSKILL improves upon SOTA performance by up to 7.1% and 8.1% on ALFWorld and WebShop. Extensive ablations and evolution analyses further validate its effectiveness in knowledge internalization and policy improvement. The code is available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/MDSKILL-CF53.

### ▫️ Knowingly Wrong: Training LLMs Against Their Own Beliefs can Induce Misalignment
**-/10** · Ariana M. Villegas-Suarez、Guido Freire、Sohan Venkatesh、Bartosz Cywiński · arXiv · 2026-11-03 · [原文](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05287v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05287v1)
*入选：能力迁移*

> 📄 **摘要**：Fine-tuning a large language model (LLM) on harmful data in a narrow domain, such as bad medical advice, can make it broadly misaligned. How realistic this risk is remains unclear, since models are very rarely fine-tuned on explicitly harmful data in practice. In this work, we show that fine-tuning on data the model believes to be false but that has no harmful content, can also induce broad misalignment. Across mathematics, trivia, cooking and sports, the more strongly the model disbelieves the answers it is trained on, the more misaligned it becomes. In mathematics, misaligned answers rise from 4% to over 60% as the training answers become more wrong, with little loss of general capability. After fine-tuning, the model states the wrong answers but its multiple-choice probabilities still prefer the true ones, which suggests that misalignment comes from this internal inconsistency. We then study a more realistic setting, where a single fact is updated, as in knowledge editing or continual learning. The same relationship holds and the more the model still believes the true fact after training on the false one, the more misaligned it becomes. When we frame the same false fact as part of a coherent story, the model comes to believe it more deeply and becomes less misaligned than when we instruct it to assert the false fact. Content filters and persona vectors cannot tell these two framings apart, even though they lead to different levels of misalignment. These results suggest a risk for model updates, where training a model that does not adopt the new fact as true can induce misalignment even when the data looks harmless.

### ▫️ De Rham cohomological architectures for PINNs and PDEs with topological holes
**-/10** · Andrew Gracyk · arXiv · 2026-11-03 · [原文](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05276v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05276v1)
*入选：领域×任务*

> 📄 **摘要**：We attempt to alleviate failure modes of classical physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) in scenarios of topological pathologies corresponding to punctures, holes, poles, and voids by introducing de Rham cohomological augmentations, or Hodge-de Rham PINNs. Vanilla PINNs often encounter pitfalls in hole-based settings due to discontinuous interfaces, nonzero closed path integration, path dependence, correspondence to exact forms via the exterior derivative, etc., therefore we absolve traditional failures via parameterizing a 1-form by the Hodge-Morrey-Friedrichs (HMF) decomposition for manifolds with boundary, drawing a connection to de Rham cohomology such as through the HMF isomorphism theorem. 1-forms can act as representations of scalar-valued PDE solutions through integral operator mappings. We propose performing a PINN-based loss on the parameterized 1-form through an associated PDE, which subsequently solves the potential-based PINN problem and moreover remedies the failures of vanilla PINNs via the Sobolev-regular operator under a Fréchet differentiation interconnection. The operator relationship often has a nontrivial closed-form and often requires integration or a case-by-case analysis, which we address thoroughly in our experiments. When an integrability condition is not met on our losses, we can equip exhaustion functions to train the networks. We demonstrate our methods on the Aharonov-Bohm effect, which, to our understanding, is largely nonexistent in PINN literature. We develop theoretical guarantees of our methods and demonstrate our pathological safeguards on a broad selection of topologically-adverse problems, including scenarios of inexact forms, and those of nontrivial harmonics and coexact terms.

### ▫️ JHiMA-WebRL: A Joint Hierarchical Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning Framework for Long-Horizon Web Tasks
**-/10** · Shanka Subhra Mondal、Jiayun Wang · arXiv · 2026-11-03 · [原文](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05263v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05263v1)
*入选：RL×LLM方法*

> 📄 **摘要**：Multimodal web agents have shown promising capabilities for interacting with websites through graphical user interfaces (GUIs), yet realistic web tasks often require long-horizon, multi-step planning that remains challenging for monolithic policies. Such agents must jointly infer high-level plans, navigate through intermediate states, track progress over extended interaction histories, and execute visually grounded actions. Although prior work has explored modular multi-agent systems with a separate planner, the planner is typically not updated jointly with the actor. We investigate whether explicitly separating and jointly training planning and execution provides a stronger inductive bias for long-horizon web navigation. We introduce JHiMA-WebRL, a joint hierarchical multi-agent reinforcement learning framework for web agents that couples a task decomposer with an actor. To train the decomposer, we use a strong teacher model to construct structured subtask supervision from multimodal GUI trajectories, and use these annotations to fine-tune both the decomposer and the subtask-conditioned actor. We then extend Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to jointly optimize the decomposer and actor in the interactive WebArena environment through hierarchical rollout grouping and role-specific advantages. On WebArena-Lite-v2, JHiMA-WebRL-14B, consisting of a 7B actor and a 7B decomposer, achieves an average success rate of 37.2%, compared with 24% for a ScaleCUA-32B agent that we fine-tune with RL on the same training tasks and evaluate under the same protocol. Ablations show that jointly updating the decomposer during RL outperforms updating the actor alone. These results suggest that jointly learned role factorization with smaller models can be a competitive alternative to scaling monolithic agents for long-horizon web tasks.

### ▫️ ARLoop: Fully-Open Pre-trained Looped Transformers with Attention Residuals
**-/10** · Yuekun Yao、Yupei Du、Dawei Zhu、Huan Sun · arXiv · 2026-11-03 · [原文](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05234v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05234v1)
*入选：能力迁移*

> 📄 **摘要**：Looped transformers increase effective depth by repeatedly applying a shared set of transformer layers, offering a parameter-efficient way to scale computation. However, standard residual connections aggregate information across recurrent depth into a single stream, providing limited control over which intermediate representations from preceding layers should be reused. In this paper, we introduce ARLoop, a looped transformer architecture based on attention residuals, where the input of each layer is selectively aggregated through attention over model depth. Distinct from standard transformers, we find that looped transformers do not require access to the full residual history: sliding-window depth attention matches or outperforms full-history attention at larger recurrent depths. Across controlled experiments with multiple parameter scales (140M and 370M) and recurrent depths (R = 1 to 5), ARLoop consistently outperforms prior residual designs for looped transformers. More importantly, we further scale ARLoop to 0.6B and 1.2B parameters under a realistic LLM pretraining recipe, demonstrating that the gains transfer to realistic pretraining, with particularly strong improvements on math and coding tasks. Our 1.2B model, pretrained on only 0.5T tokens with three recurrences, outperforms Huginn-3.5B (the only fully-open looped LLM as of our release date) across all evaluated tasks and is competitive with similar-sized non-looped models such as Qwen3.5-2B despite being trained on substantially less data.

### ▫️ DSL-Bench: Evaluating and Explaining LLM Code Generation Across Domain-Specific Languages
**-/10** · Zihan Zhou、Mingxi Ye、Yan Gao、Guipeng Wei、Zibin Zheng · arXiv · 2026-11-03 · [原文](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05168v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05168v1)
*入选：工作流×工具推理*

> 📄 **摘要**：Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used in software-engineering workflows, but code-generation benchmarks largely focus on general-purpose languages. Domain-specific languages (DSLs) have distinct syntax, semantics, and validation requirements, making results from individual DSL benchmarks difficult to compare. We present DSL-Bench, an executable benchmark and analysis framework for cross-DSL code generation. Starting from 13,045 DSL-related papers, LLM-assisted open card sorting identifies 18 representative application domains. For each domain, we select an evaluable DSL and provide a syntax checker and task-level semantic oracle. Evaluating 7 LLMs on 18 DSLs, we observe mean syntax and semantic pass rates of 31.37% and 11.64%, respectively, revealing a substantial gap between syntax acceptance and semantic correctness. A variance decomposition attributes 76.99% of semantic pass-rate variation to differences among DSLs and 7.86% to differences among models. Among four DSL-level factors, Knowledge Demand has the strongest negative association with semantic correctness (r = -0.508). We evaluate prompt engineering across all 18 DSLs and study five improvement methods on five targeted DSLs. Prompt engineering yields uneven gains; RAG-Refinement, the strongest targeted method, improves semantic correctness by 16.29 percentage points on average over matched baselines. We further derive capability profiles and DSL-Profiler summaries for interpreting data-scarce DSLs. These results highlight toolchain-specific semantic bottlenecks and the need to evaluate DSL code generation beyond syntax acceptance.

### ▫️ Meta$^2$: Precomputing Reusable Knowledge for Online Harness Evolution in Long-Horizon Agents
**-/10** · Lingzhi Yuan、Yiguang Cai、Weize Liu、Siqi Yang、Feijiang Han、Yongkyun Lee · arXiv · 2026-11-03 · [原文](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05139v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05139v1)
*入选：能力迁移*

> 📄 **摘要**：Harness optimization improves long-horizon agents by refining the prompts, tools, memory, and hooks around a frozen model. Offline methods can learn from completed trajectories, but deploy one shared harness that may overfit training tasks and cannot react to evidence revealed during a new run. Online evolution can adapt before, during, or after one run through an evolver, but may have to reason about every intervention from scratch or rely on verifier feedback that is usually unavailable at test time. We introduce Meta$^2$, which moves offline optimization one level up: rather than optimizing a shared harness, it learns reusable knowledge for harness evolution and applies it within each task online. Offline, Meta$^2$ learns a textual Meta-Skill $M$ by contrasting training rollouts of the same task agent under three configurations: without an evolver, with an evolver guided by the current version of $M$, and with an evolver given privileged verifier information available only in training. Contrasts in trajectories and outcomes are aggregated across tasks to refine $M$ into reusable guidance on when and how to evolve, when to leave the harness unchanged, and which interventions to avoid. Online, an evolver combines the frozen $M$ with emerging trajectory evidence to decide whether to KEEP the harness unchanged or EVOLVE it through a task-local edit. Each edit applies to the rest of the same run without resetting its environment or progress. Thus, evolution guidance transfers across tasks while harness edits remain task-local, and held-out verifier feedback is never available to the evolver. On two long-horizon benchmarks, Terminal-Bench 2.1 and DeepSWE 1.1, Meta$^2$ achieves the highest performance among evaluated methods with GPT-5.6-Terra, reaching 81.00% and 44.44%, respectively. Ablations support the value of contrastive evolution guidance and keeping online harness edits task-local.

### ▫️ Unifying and Beyond the Theory of Pervasive Capability Degradation in LLM Post-training SFT from Feature Interference
**-/10** · Hang Chen、Jiaying Zhu、Wenya Wang · arXiv · 2026-11-03 · [原文](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05003v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05003v1)
*入选：能力迁移*

> 📄 **摘要**：Pervasive Capability Degradation (PCD) refers to the phenomenon in which supervised fine-tuning (SFT), while adapting a large language model (LLM) to a target task, often erodes the general capabilities acquired during pre-training. Prevailing explanations attribute PCD to macroscopic distribution shift and therefore lack microscopic manipulability, leaving existing mitigation methods without a complete theoretical foundation. In this paper, we first develop a bottom-up geometric theory of PCD grounded in feature interference under superposition: narrow-domain SFT data disrupt the almost-orthogonal feature structure formed during pre-training, and the resulting interference drives activations across decision hyperplanes in the output Laguerre-Voronoi geometry, flipping predictions on general tasks. This theory unifies existing methods into six paradigms and reveals both the source of their efficacy and their theoretical limits. We then prove that cross-entropy under one-hot supervision cannot resolve PCD on its own, and accordingly propose the AEGIS loss, which directly constrains the drift of activation margins along all competing token directions, thereby decoupling interference suppression from task adaptation. Across 3 model families and 14 SFT tasks, AEGIS outperforms representative methods of every paradigm on target tasks and pervasive capabilities simultaneously, at only about 1.3 times the computational cost of standard SFT. Its generality, scalability, and mechanistic interpretability offer a new perspective on fully resolving PCD.

### ▫️ Heads That Write, Not Just Point: Image Retrieval Heads in Vision-Language Models
**-/10** · Junsung Park、Sangwon Yu、Donghun Kang、Yeongtak Oh、Jaewon Jeong、Gyeongtae Yoo · arXiv · 2026-11-03 · [原文](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04903v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04903v1)
*入选：能力迁移*

> 📄 **摘要**：Large vision-language models (LVLMs) can process interleaved text and multiple images in a single context, but how they internally identify the image relevant to a question remains poorly understood. We study this grounding step as in-context image retrieval and introduce ICIR-MCQ, a controlled probe for head-level analysis of image retrieval. Using each head's attention mass over image-token spans, we first identify sparse pointer heads that reliably point to the target image. These heads transfer beyond the probe: a single pointer head is competitive with external vision-language retrievers on a naturalistic multi-image retrieval benchmark, showing that LVLMs contain a strong internal image retrieval signal. Attention alone, however, shows where a head reads from, not what its output passes on to later layers, so we introduce a complementary output-side analysis. For each head, we train a lightweight image retriever on its value-weighted output and use it to identify writer heads, whose outputs contain enough information to identify the target image. Surprisingly, the pointer and writer criteria select substantially different head sets, with only 35 to 47 heads overlapping among the top-100 heads under each criterion. Targeted set-partition ablations show that the heads most important for standard multi-image inference are concentrated in this pointer-writer intersection, while heads selected by only one criterion show less consistent separation from random-head controls. The most consistent causal effects arise from heads selected by both the attention-based pointer and output-side writer criteria. Code and data: https://github.com/parkquasar/Image-Retrieval-Heads.

### ▫️ Amortized Nonlinear Variable Selection with Prior-Data Fitted Networks
**-/10** · Jin Zhu、Junxian Zhu · arXiv · 2026-11-03 · [原文](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04848v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04848v1)
*入选：领域×任务*

> 📄 **摘要**：Variable selection is an important task for interpreting potentially complex systems in fields such as biology, business, and sociology. The importance of variable selection has attracted considerable attention in statistics and machine learning. In this paper, we are mainly interested in model-agnostic variable selection for identifying variables that have general nonlinear dependence on the response. We propose MambaPFN, short for Model-Agnostic Markov BlAnket selection with Prior-data Fitted Networks, which is built upon the Transformer, a building block of many popular large language models. Applied to a Facebook comments dataset, MambaPFN selects the fewest variables and achieves the highest computational efficiency and predictive performance, improving upon the best baselines by about 20%.

### ▫️ Think Less, Locally: Local Preference Learning for Reasoning Compression
**-/10** · Muyao Yuan、Liyan Xu、Weizhan Zhang、Muyan Jiao、Jiangnan Li、Jinchao Zhang · arXiv · 2026-11-03 · [原文](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04814v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04814v1)
*入选：RL×LLM方法*

> 📄 **摘要**：Large reasoning models achieve strong performance on challenging tasks, but their long chain-of-thought often incurs substantial inference costs by continuing to deliberate after sufficient information has been obtained. Existing reasoning compression methods primarily operate on reasoning trajectories, paying less attention to the local decision of whether further reasoning is necessary given the current state. We propose LoCo, a local preference learning method for reasoning compression. LoCo detects candidate overthinking states from the model's own traces, freezes the corresponding reasoning prefixes, regenerates shorter verified continuations from the same states, and trains on the resulting state-conditioned preference pairs with DPO. This formulation teaches the model when to continue reasoning and when to conclude, rather than simply encouraging shorter trajectories overall. We construct training data entirely from self-generated mathematical traces and evaluate LoCo on five mathematical and three code generation benchmarks. LoCo reduces completion length by 41.7\%--45.4\% on mathematics and 23.4\%--36.3\% on code, while maintaining or improving accuracy and achieving the best AUC across all code benchmarks. LoCo consistently improves the accuracy-efficiency trade-off on mathematics and transfers effectively to code generation despite math-only training, demonstrating the cross-domain generality of local preference learning for reasoning compression. The code and model will be publicly released at https://github.com/MuyaoYuan/LoCo.
